# Pharmacy Data Analysis – CareHealthPharmacy Case Study

This repository contains a business-focused data analytics case study based on **CareHealthPharmacy**, a multi-branch retail pharmacy. The case examines sales, inventory, and data reliability challenges using realistic, messy transactional data to assess reporting accuracy and decision readiness at an **associate or senior analyst level**.

The study simulates an embedded analytics role within a scaling organization, covering end-to-end analytical ownership—from data validation and stakeholder collaboration to dashboard design and governance.

---

## 📋 Case Study: CareHealthPharmacy – Sales, Inventory & Data Reliability Assessment

### 🏢 Organization Background
CareHealthPharmacy is a growing retail pharmacy operating multiple branches and serving a diverse customer base. The organization handles high daily transaction volumes across prescription and over-the-counter products, with sales, inventory, and customer information recorded at the point of sale.

As CareHealthPharmacy scales its operations, leadership requires accurate, timely, and trustworthy data to support pricing decisions, inventory planning, branch performance evaluation, and regulatory accountability.

---

### 🎯 Analyst Role & Accountability
In this simulation, the analyst operates as a **Senior Analytics / BI Partner** embedded within CareHealthPharmacy’s Operations and Finance functions. The role is accountable for ensuring data accuracy, building trusted reporting assets, and delivering actionable insights aligned to business objectives and governance standards.

**Primary responsibilities include:**
- Designing, developing, and maintaining interactive Power BI reports and dashboards
- Building and managing data models, relationships, and calculations using Power BI Desktop, Power Query, and DAX
- Collaborating with business and technical stakeholders to gather requirements and deliver reporting solutions
- Monitoring operational metrics to avoid data integrity issues and proactively communicating changes
- Driving the adoption of self-service analytics infrastructure
- Applying data governance principles and maintaining data security and privacy across all reporting solutions

---

### 👥 Key Stakeholders & Business Functions
The analyst works closely with the following stakeholders:
- **Operations Managers** – branch performance, inventory efficiency
- **Finance / Revenue Team** – profitability, revenue accuracy
- **Inventory & Supply Chain** – stock alignment, replenishment planning
- **Executive Leadership** – strategic dashboards, decision support
- **IT / Data Engineering** – upstream data pipelines, source system integration

---

### 📊 Primary Business Use-Cases
The analytics function supports the following decision areas:
- **Daily Sales & Revenue Monitoring** – real-time visibility into branch performance
- **Inventory Replenishment Planning** – aligning stock levels with sales patterns
- **Branch Performance Benchmarking** – comparing efficiency and revenue across locations
- **Product Margin Visibility** – identifying high-margin vs. loss-making products
- **Data Quality Monitoring** – tracking completeness and accuracy of operational metrics

---

### ⚙️ Analytics Environment (Simulated)
**Source Systems:**
- Point-of-Sale (POS) transactional data
- Inventory management logs
- Customer and product master data

**Data Preparation Layer:**
- SQL for validation, aggregation, and consistency checks
- Power Query (M) for extraction, transformation, and loading (ETL)
- Data model relationships and DAX calculations

**BI & Reporting Layer:**
- Power BI Desktop (development)
- Power BI Service (deployment, scheduling, sharing)
- Role-based security and row-level security (RLS) implementation

**Governance & Documentation:**
- Metric definitions and validation rules
- Refresh schedules and performance optimization
- Clear distinction between *trusted* vs. *estimated* metrics

**Project & Collaboration Tools:**

### 🔍 Business Situation
Despite steady sales activity, CareHealthPharmacy faces increasing difficulty answering fundamental business questions with confidence:
- Are reported sales figures accurate?
- Which products are truly profitable?
- Are stock levels aligned with actual sales?
- Which branches and sales representatives are performing well?

Operational teams have raised concerns that inconsistencies in transaction data may be distorting performance reports and inventory decisions.

---

### ❗ Business Problem Statement
CareHealthPharmacy lacks reliable visibility into sales performance, profitability, and inventory efficiency due to inconsistent and incomplete transactional data.

Current reporting processes depend heavily on manual data entry and post-hoc reconciliation, leading to:
- Conflicting revenue numbers across reports
- Unclear profit margins for key products
- Stock discrepancies between system records and physical inventory
- Limited trust in executive dashboards

Leadership requires a structured analytical assessment before investing further in expansion, automation, or advanced reporting.

---

### 🎯 Engagement Objective
CareHealthPharmacy is engaging a **Senior / Associate Data Analyst** to conduct an independent analytical assessment of the pharmacy’s transactional data in order to:
1. Determine whether current data can be trusted for decision-making
2. Identify critical data gaps affecting revenue, margin, and inventory reporting
3. Establish a reliable analytical foundation for future dashboards and operational insights

---

### 📦 Analyst Scope of Work (Expected Deliverables)
The analyst is expected to:
1. Evaluate the completeness and consistency of sales and inventory data
2. Assess the impact of missing or inconsistent fields on financial reporting
3. Establish standardized business metrics for sales, revenue, and profitability
4. Provide leadership with a clear understanding of what insights can and cannot be derived from the current data

The engagement is diagnostic in nature and focuses on **analytical validation** rather than system implementation.

---

### 📁 Data Context
The analyst will be provided with transaction-level pharmacy data including:
- Product sales quantities and prices
- Buying prices and total transaction amounts
- Branch and sales representative identifiers
- Inventory stock levels
- Customer identifiers and payment methods

The dataset reflects real operational conditions, including incomplete records, inconsistent formatting, and manual entry errors.

---

### 🛠️ Tools & Skill Expectations
CareHealthPharmacy expects the analyst to independently select appropriate tools to complete the assessment. Typical tools may include:
- SQL for data validation, aggregation, and consistency checks
- Excel / Power Query for data inspection and reconciliation
- Power BI (or similar BI tools) for structured metric evaluation and visualization

Tool choice is secondary to analytical judgment, accuracy, and business understanding.

---

### ✅ Success Criteria
This engagement will be considered successful if leadership can:
- Clearly understand the reliability of current sales and inventory data
- Identify key risks caused by data quality issues
- Confidently decide next steps regarding reporting, inventory controls, and analytics investment

**Success measures for the analytics function:**
- Increased trust in dashboards and reported metrics
- Reduced reporting discrepancies across teams
- Faster decision turnaround through self-service reporting
- Improved inventory alignment and reduction in stock discrepancies
- Higher adoption of BI tools across business units

---

### 🧠 Why This Case Matters
CareHealthPharmacy operates in a highly margin-sensitive and inventory-critical environment. Without reliable data, even strong sales performance can conceal inefficiencies, losses, and operational risk.

This case study represents a realistic business problem requiring **senior-level analytical thinking**, **validation rigor**, and **stakeholder-focused insight**—competencies essential for roles in data analytics, business intelligence, and operational decision support.
